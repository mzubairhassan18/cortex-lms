The problem

Learning in a chat window is a scroll of forgetting.

The tools we use to ask questions were built for one-off answers, not for understanding something over a week. So you re-read, re-ask and re-scroll — and the tab count grows while the understanding doesn't.

↑

The good answer is twelve messages up

You get a reply that finally makes sense. Then twelve more messages bury it, and going back for it costs you the thought you were holding.

✎

Your notes live somewhere else

The useful sentence ends up in a doc or a sticky note, detached from the conversation it came from. Later, neither the note nor the chat makes sense on its own.

?

Nothing checks whether it stuck

You read it and felt good about it. A week later you can't reproduce the idea — and there was never a moment that would have told you.

What Cortex does

The whole topic, on one screen.

Every feature below is a direct answer to one of those three problems — which is why they all live in the same place.

Explain beside the question

So the answer stops scrolling away.

Select any sentence and press Explain. It opens in its own panel next to the conversation, with its own thread — ask follow-ups there without derailing the main chat.

A graph of what you learned

So you can see the shape of a topic.

Switch to the graph view: your conversations and every explanation branch out as a horizontal tree. Zoom it, drag around it, and watch the subject spread out instead of vanishing into a scroll bar.

Notes that keep their source

So a note still means something later.

Save a passage from any conversation. The session summary gathers your topics and notes in one place, so you can review a subject without re-reading the chat.

Quizzes from your own conversation

So "I understood it" becomes provable.

Cortex writes questions from what you actually discussed — not a generic question bank — and shows which parts didn't land, so you know what to re-open.

It remembers

So picking up is instant.

Conversations, explanations, notes and test results persist between sessions. Close the lid tonight, open it tomorrow, carry on mid-thought.

Free models, or bring your own key

So starting costs nothing.

Begin on free keyless models with no sign-up. When you want more, add an OpenAI-compatible key of your own — it stays on your server and is never shared with anyone else.

How it works

Three moves, on repeat.

  1. 1

    Ask

    Chat like you already do. Attach a PDF, a Word file or a link and it becomes part of that conversation's context.

  2. 2

    Explain

    Highlight whatever didn't land and open it beside the chat. Branch it again if you need to — explanations nest, and the tree stays tidy.

  3. 3

    Prove it

    Read the summary, skim your notes, take the quiz. Anything you missed points straight back at the explanation that will fix it.

The fine print, up front

What's actually true about it.

No testimonials and no user counts — this is a new product, and inventing social proof would be the first lie you read. Here is what you can check instead.

  • No account to start. Open it and ask something.
  • No subscription. The product itself is free to run.
  • Bring your own key. Your API key stays on your own server and is used only for your requests.
  • Free models are built in. You can learn an entire topic without paying anyone anything.
  • Your data is yours. Conversations, notes and files are stored where you installed it.
  • It runs locally. One command, your own machine, no cloud lock-in.
